Understanding European Soccer

European soccer can be confusing if you are new to the sport. Learn how leagues, promotion and relegation, European competitions, and common soccer terms work.

League System

How Soccer Leagues Work

European soccer clubs compete in domestic leagues within their own countries. Unlike many American sports leagues, teams normally play each opponent more than once during a season and earn points based on their results.

A team receives three points for a win, one point for a draw, and zero points for a loss. The points are added throughout the season to create the league table.

The team with the most points at the end of the season normally becomes the league champion. If teams finish level on points, rules such as goal difference may be used to separate them.

England

The Premier League is the highest level of the English soccer league system.

Spain

La Liga

La Liga is the top professional soccer division in Spain.

Germany

Bundesliga

The Bundesliga is the highest level of professional soccer in Germany.

Italy

Serie A

Serie A is the top level of the Italian soccer league system.

Moving Between Leagues

Promotion and Relegation

One major difference between European soccer and many American professional sports is promotion and relegation. Clubs can move between different levels of their country's soccer system depending on how well they perform.

Teams that finish near the bottom of a top league may be relegated to the division below it. At the same time, successful teams from the lower division can be promoted to replace them.

This system makes the bottom of the league table important because teams are not only competing for championships. Some clubs are also fighting to remain in the highest division.

Beyond Domestic Soccer

European Competitions

Clubs can also qualify for competitions that include teams from different European countries. Qualification usually depends on a club's performance in its domestic league or cup competitions.

These tournaments allow clubs from countries such as England, Spain, Germany, and Italy to compete against one another during the same season.

🏆

UEFA Champions League

The Champions League is Europe's most prestigious club competition and features many of the continent's strongest teams.

⚽

UEFA Europa League

The Europa League is another major European tournament that gives clubs from across Europe an opportunity to compete for an international trophy.

🌍

UEFA Conference League

The Conference League provides another European competition for clubs that qualify through their domestic competitions.

Cup Competitions

Domestic Cups

League competitions are not the only trophies available to European clubs. Countries also organize domestic cup competitions that are separate from the regular league season.

Cup tournaments normally use knockout matches. A club that loses can be eliminated from the competition, while the winner advances toward the final.

Italy

Italy's main domestic knockout cup competition.

Soccer Vocabulary

Common Soccer Terms

Knowing a few common terms makes European soccer easier to understand. These words are regularly used when discussing clubs, leagues, and competitions.

League

A season-long competition where clubs earn points based on match results.

Table

The standings that show each team's position, points, wins, draws, and losses.

Derby

A match between clubs with a strong local or regional rivalry.

Goal Difference

The difference between the number of goals a team scores and the number it allows.

Clean Sheet

A match in which a team does not allow its opponent to score a goal.

Hat-Trick

When one player scores three goals in the same match.

Transfer

The process of a player moving from one soccer club to another.

Extra Time

Additional playing time used in some knockout matches when the score is tied after regulation.
